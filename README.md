# Ethiopia's Digital Inclusion — Visual System & Insight

A portfolio project that turns World Bank Open Data on Ethiopia’s digital connectivity into a clear visual system, a one-page insight brief, and social media adaptations.

**Role demonstrated:** Visual & Data Analyst  
**Focus:** Analysis + visual design ownership for an institutional audience

---

## Project Overview

Ethiopia has seen rapid growth in mobile subscriptions, yet internet penetration still lags regional peers. This project explores that gap, the mobile-first nature of access, and the relationship between connectivity and economic output — then communicates the findings through restrained, decision-oriented visuals.

**Key questions answered:**
- How has mobile vs internet adoption evolved in Ethiopia (2010–2024)?
- How does Ethiopia compare with the Sub-Saharan Africa average?
- What does the structure of access (mobile vs fixed) imply for policy?
- How does internet penetration relate to economic output?

## Key Insights

- Mobile subscriptions have grown dramatically, while internet penetration has increased more slowly — creating a structural adoption gap.
- Mobile networks account for the vast majority of connections, making mobile infrastructure and affordability the decisive inclusion levers.
- Ethiopia still trails the Sub-Saharan Africa average on internet penetration, though recent acceleration is visible.
- Higher internet penetration is associated with stronger economic output across the region, reinforcing digital access as a growth priority.

---

## Data & Sources

- World Bank Open Data
- Period covered: 2010–2024 (where available)

Indicators used include mobile cellular subscriptions, internet users / penetration, fixed broadband, and GDP per capita.

---

## Limitations

- Correlation between internet penetration and GDP per capita does not imply causation.
- Mobile subscription figures can include multiple SIM ownership and do not equal unique users.
- Internet penetration definitions and collection methods can vary slightly across countries and years.
- Some series contain gaps or lagged reporting; analysis uses the best available comparable data.
- Regional averages mask significant variation within Sub-Saharan Africa.

---

## Design Decisions

- **Restrained palette** — Deep slate navy + warm terracotta accent for institutional credibility and clear emphasis.
- **Direct labelling** preferred over heavy legends to reduce cognitive load.
- **Muted comparison series** so Ethiopia’s data remains the focal point.
- **One-page constraint** forced prioritisation of the most decision-relevant findings.
- **Reusable visual system** so future charts and reports can stay consistent.
